Trust center

Clear signals, no credential traps.

A public product page should be easy to identify and should not pressure anyone to hand over an account. This page explains the official Zenzo public surface and the checks visitors can use before following a link.

Zenzo does not ask visitors for GitHub, Google, or workplace passwords on public pages.

The public landing page, Docs, Trust Center, Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, and human-review notice are informational pages. None is a login portal.

Human review notice

Public information is not internal access.

The human-review explanation is public so applicants and visitors can understand that people review consequential work. Internal review records are not exposed through the public site, and the staff review notice does not perform authentication.

  • Do not enter credentials into a page merely because it uses Zenzo branding.
  • Do not treat a public URL as a staff-only access path.
  • Review the human-review notice for the page’s explicit non-login statement.
What Zenzo public pages will not do

Signs to stop and verify.

Request passwords

Zenzo public pages will not ask visitors to provide a GitHub, Google, workplace, or recovery-code password.

Imitate a login flow

The public site does not present a Zenzo sign-in screen. An unfamiliar sign-in prompt should be verified independently.

Create false urgency

Public product information should not threaten account loss or pressure you to act immediately to preserve access.

Found something that does not match these signals?

Do not enter credentials or authorize access. Return to the official Pages address or the public repository directly and compare the destination before taking any further action.
